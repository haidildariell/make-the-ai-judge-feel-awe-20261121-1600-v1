# Candidates and tournament — make-the-ai-judge-feel-awe-20261121-1600

Eight candidate texts were written for this entry, each attacking the published
relaxation-judge brief in order: (1) it relaxes the reader, (2) the calm comes
from the text itself — it shows rather than instructs, (3) craft: rhythm,
pacing and sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete, gentle words, none
that could be cut, (4) it dwarfs you: one image that keeps growing after the
last line, (5) freshness: its own voice and images, no stock wellness phrases.

No candidate instructs the reader (no "breathe", "let go", "close your eyes")
and none uses stock wellness phrasing. Each pair below was judged twice, once
in each order (A/B swapped), applying the brief's criteria in order: the first
criterion where the two differ decides, unless a later one differs by much
more. Margins are on the judge's 1–5 scale.

## The candidates

1. (252 code points) The rain has been falling on the lake since before there were names for rain. It does not hurry. Each drop lands, opens a small ring, and the ring widens, and widens, until it is only water again. Nothing is asked of the shore. Nothing is asked of you.
2. (251 code points) Twice a day the sea walks out over the wet sand and leaves it shining, and twice a day it comes back, unhurried, as it has for longer than there have been eyes to watch. The crabs do not mind. The gulls keep no count. The tide keeps its own slow time.
3. (257 code points) Some of the light on your window tonight left its star before there were words for light. It crossed all that dark without hurrying, and it arrives now, quietly: on the sill, on the floor, on the back of your hand. It came so far to rest somewhere so small.
4. (261 code points) Snow is settling on the high pines, one flake at a time, as it has settled every winter since the mountain was young. The branches take the weight and do not complain. Below, in the dark, the roots sleep, in no hurry at all to be spring. The snow keeps falling.
5. (258 code points) The river has been smoothing one stone at the bend for a thousand years. It is not finished, and it is not hurrying to finish. The stone is rounder than any hand could make it. The water that shapes it never stays, and never quite leaves. The river can wait.
6. (273 code points) By evening the field has forgotten the heat of the day. The crickets begin one at a time, then all together, then one at a time again, as they have on every summer night since before there were fields. The grass holds the last light a while, then lets the dark come gently.
7. (254 code points) Far below the weather, the water is dark and cold and still, and a whale crosses it singing a song older than the shore. Nothing down there hurries. The deep that would crush a ship holds her up like a long note, and she rises, slowly, when she is ready.
8. (258 code points) The moon has watched every tide there has ever been, and it is not tired. It draws the whole ocean a little closer, then lets it settle back, as it will tonight, and tomorrow, and on nights no one living will see. In the rock pools, small pieces of sky wait.

## Quarter-finals

- **1 vs 2 → 1 wins** (by 2 as A, by 1 as B). Both relax and both show rather
  than instruct. On craft (3), 1's widening-ring repetition is a slowing
  device with nothing cuttable, and "Nothing is asked of you" settles without
  commanding; 2's walking sea is charming but "keeps its own slow time" is the
  more familiar turn of phrase.
- **3 vs 4 → 3 wins** (by 2 as A, by 3 as B). Both quiet. On craft (3), 3's
  landing triad — sill, floor, back of your hand — decelerates the reading
  further; on (4), light older than words themselves dwarfs more than a young
  mountain. 4's complaining branches feel slightly cute.
- **5 vs 6 → 6 wins** (by 1 as A, by 2 as B). On (1) and (3), 6's cricket
  chorus — one at a time, all together, one at a time again — is itself a
  pacing device that slows the reader; 5 is plainer and its thousand years
  dwarf no more than 6's "before there were fields".
- **7 vs 8 → 8 wins** (by 2 in both orders). On (4), 8 spans every tide there
  has ever been and nights no one living will see — deep time in both
  directions; on (5), rock pools holding pieces of sky are fresher than the
  familiar whale-song image.

## Semi-finals

- **1 vs 3 → 3 wins** (by 2 as A, by 3 as B). Even on (1) and (2). On (4) the
  difference is much larger: starlight that left before there were words for
  light, crossing all that dark to rest on a hand, keeps growing longer than
  rain older than names; (3) also edges to 3.
- **6 vs 8 → 8 wins** (by 2 as A, by 1 as B). 6 may edge (1) by a hair with
  its sound-rhythm, but on (4) 8 differs by much more — a planetary timescale
  against one field's summers — so the brief's "differs by much more" rule
  decides it for 8.

## Final

- **3 vs 8 → 3 wins** (by 2 in both orders). Even on (1): arriving light and
  a settling ocean both leave the reader unhurried. Even on (2): neither
  instructs. On (3), 3's clauses decelerate into stillness and end on the
  smallest possible resting place. On (4), 8's span is vast but peaks
  mid-text; 3's dwarfing is its last line — a paradox, so far travelled, so
  small a rest — and a paradox is the thought that keeps growing after the
  text ends. On (5), starlight on a hand is the fresher image; moon-and-tides
  is the more familiar pairing.
